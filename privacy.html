Privacy Policy

Meep · Effective September 3, 2026

Meep does not track you, and shares nothing with anybody. There is no advertising, no analytics service, and no third-party SDK in the app. What it stores is what two people need in order to play the same board and compare scores honestly.

Your account

On first launch your device generates a random 32-byte secret and keeps it in the iOS Keychain. The server stores only its SHA-256, so a copy of the database is not a list of ways to sign in as its own rows. You are never asked to create an account and never shown a login screen.

Sign in with Apple

Optional, and only if you choose it. Meep receives the identifier Apple issues for this app and nothing else — it does not request your name or your email address. It exists so an account can survive a new phone.

Your handle

Every player has one. Meep deals you a generated handle (something like @swiftotter) when your account is created, and you can change it any time in Settings. It is public and searchable by design: a handle is how someone finds you to send a board.

Your photo

Optional. If you set one, it is chosen through the system photo picker — Meep never asks for access to your photo library — resized on your device, stored with your account, and shown to other players next to your handle. You can change or remove it any time from your profile, and deleting your account deletes it. If you never set one, Meep shows a colored circle with your initials instead, and no photo exists anywhere.

Your phone number and contacts

Adding your phone number is optional. If you add it, your phone turns it into a fingerprint (a one-way code) and sends only that; Meep keeps the fingerprint, never the number, only for as long as you keep your number added, and deletes it the moment you remove it or delete your account. A fingerprint of a phone number does not hide it completely — a phone number has too few possible values, so anyone holding Meep’s data and the formula behind the fingerprint could work a number out by trying every one. This protects your number against casual disclosure, not against someone determined to reverse it.

Contacts are read only when you tap Find friends from your contacts, and only the contacts you allow — iOS can limit this to a handful. Names never leave your phone. The numbers are turned into fingerprints on your phone and sent to ask which belong to Meep players who also added their own number; the answer is what you see on screen. Meep keeps none of it: no contact of yours is stored, and nothing records who you know. The one thing written down is a count of how many times your account has asked today — no numbers, no names, just a number of requests — which is how Meep holds this to at most 4,000 numbers a day per account. Nothing about your contacts reaches another player unless you tap Invite or Challenge.

Inviting a contact who is not on Meep opens your phone’s own Messages app with a link already typed in. You send it — Meep does not, and never sends a text on your behalf.

You can remove your number at any time from the You tab’s Friends card: Your number → Remove. From that moment you can no longer be found by it.

Meep has no way to tell you whether a number you add already belongs to another player: if somebody else added that number first, their having it does not make you findable.

Rounds you play against other people

Meep stores the score and a log of the words and timings, which the server replays to check that a claimed score is one the board could actually produce. This is what makes a competitive result mean anything. Rounds you play on your own stay on your device.

Reports

If you report someone, Meep stores the reason and any note you write, so that a person can read it.

Network address

Your address is used briefly to apply rate limits and handle abuse. It is not used to build a profile and is not joined to anything else.

Deleting your account

In the app: Settings → Delete account. It destroys every credential, session and device record, so nothing can sign in as you again, clears your handle and display name, deletes your photo, and forgets the fingerprint of your phone number if you had added one.

One thing deliberately survives. Games you have already finished stay in your opponents’ history, without your name on them — a completed challenge is two people’s record, and the other player’s result is not ours to delete. Reports are kept for the same reason.

Children

Meep is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect their data.

Contact

Questions, or a request about your data: github.com/DaveJabe/Meep/issues.
